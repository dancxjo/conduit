# Body time

BodyTime is a Body-scoped coordinate derived from identified Host-local monotonic clocks. It is not UTC, a scheduler tick broadcast by one permanent leader, or a causal order. A conversion retains its local Host, Boot, and clock basis; Body basis and correlation generation; earliest, center, and latest possible ticks; correlation age; and source policy/provenance.

## Admission and continuity

- `BodyClockCorrelation` projects one local basis into BodyTime with a bounded rate error, anchor uncertainty, and maximum age. `BodyTimeRequirement` admits the current estimate and the operation's future horizon only when both fit the Plan's tolerance. Transport and scheduler uncertainty can be reserved explicitly from that tolerance.
- `BodyClockRateEstimator` retains finite accepted and rejected evidence. A new generation must agree with prior bounded intervals and a plausible rate. `BodyTimeTracker` refuses a reconciliation that would reverse already admitted local progress. A partition widens projected uncertainty until quality degrades or becomes unsupported.
- `BodyClockPeerAdmission` requires current Body membership, an admitted source policy, and exact Host/Boot proof. `BodyClockLine` carries four monotonic samples and the source correlation in protected request/reply frames; its TCP adapter applies bounded framing and a monotonic round-trip deadline. Establishing the protected session requires an independently provisioned peer-specific preshared key and fresh ephemeral entropy. Mere reachability is not clock authority.
- A first authorized peer can bootstrap a local correlation. Changing peer, Boot, membership revision, or policy requires a new admission. No old event observation is rewritten when a later correlation improves.
- A BodyTime-qualified `BodyPlan` can admit clock evidence for every exact Host/Boot participating in its Plot fragments or Mask chains. Admission refuses missing, duplicate, unknown, stale, or out-of-tolerance evidence and retains per-Host correlation, quality, and execution bounds. The standard Host runner consumes a matching Plan-bound local admission and rechecks its current clock before execution. Its remote-fragment preparation entry point checks exact Plan membership and current local quality before reservation; qualified remote stepping refuses regression, basis replacement, stale quality, or unavailable clocks and latches quality loss until re-admission. Direct kernel stepping cannot bypass that gate. Neither path by itself proves that remote fragments were dispatched or executed.

## Separate truths

Physical-time comparison returns `Before`, `After`, or `Indeterminate` from non-overlapping or overlapping intervals. A causal send/receive edge is retained independently of those estimates. The same event record can carry local order, an optional at-event BodyTime estimate, its original correlation, and causal predecessors. Replayed estimates must still match that correlation. A sample captured only after an event is marked `AfterEvent` and does not claim to timestamp that event.

For civil work, a caller supplies an explicit timezone/rule resolution, including fold or gap choice. `CivilDeadlineAdmission` retains that choice, the wall correlation, and one exact monotonic deadline. Its standard-host wait observes that deadline's Host/Boot/basis; later wall steps neither re-resolve the occurrence nor fire the wait early. Reboot or basis replacement refuses the old deadline.

## Scheduling and clock inspection

Elapsed operations consume durations on their admitted monotonic provider, not UTC or local civil values. The portable API separates `MonotonicDuration`, `MonotonicInstant`, and `MonotonicDeadline` from wall `TemporalInstant`, local date/time, and `ZonedResolution`. Cross-basis arithmetic needs an explicit correlation. Provider-local counters in an already selected Host Call are relative to that exact possession; they are not portable deadlines that another Host can consume as bare milliseconds.

A BodyTime requirement reserves current uncertainty, drift through the operation horizon, and declared transport/scheduler uncertainty before execution. Qualified remote fragments recheck that requirement before each scheduler step. `RemoteBodyTimeStepRefusal::Quality` retains the exact `Degrading` or `Unsupported` result, and loss remains latched until a new admission; calling the underlying ordinary step cannot bypass the gate. This is a refusal to advance, not an automatic retry, wall-clock fallback, or hard real-time guarantee.

Operator causal inspection exposes the retained local sequence alongside its evidence identity, Host, Boot, Play, and Placement, separately from physical bounds and explicit causal edges. Sequence is a finite `u32` within the original report's kernel collector, not a Body-wide clock. Comparing sequences requires provenance establishing the same report/collector; matching an evidence execution digest and Host/Boot alone is insufficient. Collectors refuse `SignError::SequenceOverflow` before wrapping; a replacement collector starts a new local stream rather than continuing the exhausted counter. Numbers from different streams establish neither local order nor causality. No logical, hybrid, or vector clock is introduced.

`explain_clock_quality` projects the authoritative quality returned by a runner report or a remote fragment's `clock_quality()` into inspection data. It preserves ready/degrading/unsupported state and the typed refusal. Authorized operator detail includes local and Body bases, generation, age, bounds, and source policy; public inspection keeps the warning while withholding correlation identities. Admission references and transport credentials are never copied into this projection. Civil deadline accessors separately expose the original resolved occurrence, wall correlation identity, and exact monotonic deadline basis. An absent event-time sample is not a claim that the event was synchronized.

## Corrections, suspension, and lifecycle

Civil admission chooses one resolved occurrence and adds its admitted uncertainty conservatively before producing a monotonic deadline. An unresolved or mismatched fold/gap, different wall basis/scale, excessive uncertainty, excessive horizon, or non-future occurrence is a typed refusal. Any size of later wall correction leaves that deadline unchanged. Choosing a different civil occurrence requires an explicit new obligation; a wait result does not create another scheduler effect or authorize replay of an already completed request.

The supplied clock profiles do not establish a portable guarantee that suspend time is counted. Ordinary elapsed timing follows the selected provider: if its counter pauses, the deadline remains pending for the remaining provider duration; if it advances beyond the deadline, a delayed wake completes the existing request. A regressing, replaced, expired, or unavailable provider refuses instead of inventing elapsed time. Reboot/process or browser-session replacement changes the identified local basis and invalidates old deadlines even when counter numbers coincide.

BodyTime continuity across suspension requires a separately admitted bound on that provider's sleep behavior. The current std/browser/ConduitOS profiles have no measured sleep bound and must not be admitted as preserving synchronization through arbitrary suspend/resume. An owner that cannot retain its admitted error model across suspension must stop qualified execution and obtain new correlation evidence/admission before resuming it. Paused-counter age alone cannot account for time spent asleep. This limitation is distinct from a delayed callback on a continuously valid clock; none of the local fixtures below proves physical suspend/resume synchronization.

## Profile and proof boundaries

The standard Host exposes an identified process-scoped `ThreadTimer` basis. Browser timer sessions expose a distinct `performance.now()` basis and refuse a lost or regressing provider; the browser Host can return a basis-qualified local sample when its clock resource is acquired. A typed ConduitOS monotonic observation exists for its supported x86 path. A target without an admitted identified clock remains unsupported rather than using wall time.

The deterministic multi-Host core fixture covers offset origins, drift, partition, rejoin, reboot, wall steps, faulty peer observations, overlapping intervals, and causal send/receive. Standard-host fixtures admit deliberately offset Host clocks under one Body Plan, prepare exact fragments through the qualified path, and route values through the installed remote-Cord kernel. One fixture executes a `time/tick` Plot across two Host fragments, completes the receiver's observer call, and drains its scheduler while both clock projections stay within admitted bounds. These Host fixtures run in one process with simulated clocks and an in-process Line; they do not prove physical distribution or real-time delivery. Other focused standard-host tests cover protected TCP exchange, actual wire handshake, two separate native processes with distinct monotonic bases, key mismatch, and malformed or slow frames. Chromium and a native process exercise heterogeneous timer bases and BodyTime correlation on one physical machine. The civil fixture exercises an explicit DST fold or gap choice followed by monotonic waiting.

These tests do not demonstrate a shared BodyTime-qualified Plot executing on two physical Hosts, a hosted/public deployment, or an independent real-world synchronization guarantee. The TCP process fixture uses test membership and a test preshared key; production provisioning and cross-Host Plot orchestration must be proved separately before claiming those outcomes.
