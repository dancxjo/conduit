Chromium · seven steps

Try a clock, inspect it, and keep your changes

Open your local Handbook, check and run a clock, inspect its resident Patchbay, edit its timing, then lull and recover the same Body.

Before you begin

Open your Handbook in a browser with local storage enabled. This recording uses a fresh Chromium profile and static files. Each browser installation creates its own local Body; no shared application backend is required.

  1. Open your own Handbook

    You do: Open the static Handbook in a fresh browser profile.

    The Handbook creates a local Body, then wakes its resident application. The page offers lessons, editable source, and lifecycle controls.

  2. Check an unfinished edit

    You do: Open Start here, choose the clock lesson, enter unfinished source, and press Try in my Handbook.

    Live highlighting follows the Unicode edit. The checker reports a refusal; the existing Body, Play, and installed workset remain unchanged.

  3. Run the clock lesson

    You do: Restore the clock source and press Try in my Handbook.

    The checked clock joins the same Body and runs through a newly admitted Plan and Play.

  4. Inspect the running clock

    You do: Press Open in Patchbay, choose the clock, focus its output port, and press Enter.

    The resident Patchbay shows the clock's gears, ports, and cord. Its selected output port and graph identities belong to this Body's actual Plan and Play.

  5. Change the clock's timing

    You do: Change time/every(1s) to time/every(2s), then press Try in my Handbook.

    The same Body replaces the clock with the newly checked source and admits a new Plan and Play. The workset does not grow with a duplicate clock.

  6. Bring the Body to rest

    You do: After checking that the edit survives switching lessons and inspecting the new graph, press Lull.

    Execution ends and Wake becomes available. The Body retains the edited clock without an active Play.

  7. Return to your edited clock

    You do: Press Wake, then reload the page.

    The same Host and Body return with a fresh Boot, Plan, and Play. The two-second source, installed workset, and foreground selection survive.

What stayed with you?

The Body kept its edited clock and workset through a reload. The new Boot admitted fresh execution; the recording does not claim the clock ran while the page was closed.

Try it in your own Handbook, or follow a smaller clock journey.
