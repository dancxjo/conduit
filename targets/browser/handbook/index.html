Try your first example ↓

Your first experiment

  1. Predict. Choose “A clock you can stop.” Read the source: time/every(1s) sets the interval; >> connects its ticks to the display.
  2. Try. Choose Try in my Handbook and watch the ticks. Change 1s to 2s, try again, and compare the pace.
  3. Look inside. Open Patchbay and select the clock from the resident plots. Select a gear, port, or cord in its diagram to inspect what it means.
  4. Stop and return. Choose Lull to stop execution. Wake starts it again. A reload recovers the same local body while admitting fresh execution.

Put words to what you saw

A plot describes the work. Gears invoke kinds of operation; cords connect their typed ports. A plan selects how the work will run, and a play executes it.

Yours, in this browser

The site delivers the starting material; your browser creates this body and keeps its continuity locally. A different browser gets a different body. Clearing application storage can lose your local body. Inspect storage status or start over under Your body and browser.

The chapters are ordinary documents. The interactive examples run through the local runtime; they do not need a shared backend.
